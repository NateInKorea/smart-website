The same score can come from very different reasons.

"I think they just can't focus."
"They'd do better if they just tried harder."
"I don't think they know how to study yet."

But it's hard to tell the real reason just by looking at the outside.

A child might not yet know how to make a plan. Or the way they're being asked to study simply doesn't fit them. Some children lose confidence quickly after a setback; others' focus depends heavily on their surroundings.

"How is this child actually learning?"

Before judging a child, we try to understand how they learn.

Seven questions that explain how your child learns

SMART LQ doesn't simply sort children into "good at school" or "not." Across 7 domains, it looks at both a child's strengths and where they could use support.

① Metacognitive Awareness
"Do I know how I actually study?"
Making a plan, checking your own understanding, and reflecting on how you studied.
② Motivation
"What actually makes me want to study?"
Curiosity, growth, achievement, praise, or reward — whatever starts and sustains the effort.
③ Processing Preference
"How do I understand new things best?"
Seeing, hearing, reading, or trying it hands-on — the way new information actually sinks in.
④ Feedback Responsiveness
"How do I react when I'm told I got it wrong?"
The attitude behind accepting correction and actually using it next time.
⑤ Learning Environment
"Where do I actually focus best?"
Noise, space, people, routine — the conditions that affect real concentration.
⑥ Persistence
"Can I try again, even when it's hard?"
Accepting mistakes and setbacks, and trying again anyway.
⑦ Language Learning
"How do I learn and actually use a new language?"
From the willingness to speak and make mistakes, to noticing patterns and putting them to real use.

Try one for yourself, rather than just reading about it.

There's no right or wrong answer. The goal isn't to answer like a "good student" — just pick whatever's closest to how you actually are when you study.

One assessment, four perspectives

What a student needs to know and what a parent needs to know are not the same thing. SMART LQ shows the same result differently to the student, the parent, the teacher, and the school.

Student

How should I study?

Understand your own strengths and habits, and find a way of studying that actually fits you.

Parent

How can I help my child?

Instead of "try harder," discover the specific environment and support your child actually needs.

Teacher

What teaching approach works for this student?

Understand how a student learns, and use it in lessons, feedback, and individual guidance.

School

How are our students learning, overall?

See patterns across students and apply them to teaching and support — without naming names.

"Are they bad at studying — or is the method just wrong for them?"

Teaching children, you see the same explanation land completely differently from one child to the next. One understands immediately. Another needs to see it drawn out. Another doesn't really get it until they've tried it themselves.

Watching that, one question kept coming back: before telling a child to try harder, what if we could actually understand how they learn?

SMART LQ was built to go one step further than measuring "how well did you do" — toward understanding "how do you actually learn."

Rather than judging a child by a number, understand how they learn. Rather than only looking for what's missing, show them what to try next. That's why we built SMART LQ.
