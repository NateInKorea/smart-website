Seven domains, not one score

Every SMART LQ assessment measures the same seven areas of how a person learns — the score is a starting point, not the whole picture. Each domain, and every tip built from it, is grounded in established learning-science research rather than invented from scratch.

Metacognitive Awareness
Whether a student plans ahead, checks their own understanding, and reflects on mistakes rather than just moving past them.
Motivation Orientation
What actually drives a student to keep going — curiosity, personal growth, rewards, or recognition from others.
Processing Preference
How a student takes in new material best — through images, hearing it explained, hands-on trial, or careful reading.
Feedback Responsiveness
How a student reacts to correction — as fuel for improvement, or as something to brace against.
Learning Environment Needs
The conditions a student actually needs to concentrate — quiet versus noise, routine versus flexibility, alone versus with others.
Persistence Beliefs
Whether a student treats difficulty as a sign to try a different approach, or a sign to stop.
Language Learning Style
How comfortably a student takes risks, notices patterns, and builds real communication in a second language.

The same score, four different views

SMART LQ doesn't just report a number — it tells each person in a student's life what to actually do with it.

Student

How you learn best, explained in your own terms.

Parent

Specific ways to help your child at home.

Teacher

How to teach this particular student well.

School

Patterns across a class, without naming names.

Beyond the score

SMART LQ isn't a one-time test — it's built to keep being useful over time.

Growth tracking

Every retake adds to a running history, so parents and teachers can see whether a domain is actually improving, not just what today's score says.

Private teacher notes

Teachers can log short, domain-tagged observations about a student. Only that teacher and the student's parent ever see them — not other teachers, not the school.

Tips you can act on

Every tip can be marked as tried, and gentle reminders nudge parents and teachers toward the ones they haven't gotten to yet — not another notification to ignore.

An honest methodology

Each of the 7 domains is grounded in peer-reviewed research. We say plainly what SMART LQ is — and isn't: not a clinical or diagnostic tool.

Every subject, not just English

The tip library spans 25 school subjects at three levels each, all shaped by the same 7-domain profile — math and science get guidance just as specific as language learning does.

How it works

1

Take the assessment

70 questions, written for either Young Learners (ages 7–12) or Older Learners (13+), in English or Korean. Takes about 20 minutes.

2

Get the LQ score

A single 300–900 score, plus a full breakdown across all seven domains — not just a number, but where it comes from.

3

See what to do with it

Parents, teachers, and schools each get their own tailored guidance, drawn from the same research-backed domains — not generic advice, and not a clinical diagnosis.
